## Problem Statement
Interns and residents in Bangladesh make treatment decisions whose consequences show up hours or days later: hypokalemia after insulin, AKI after nephrotoxic drugs, rebleeding after under-resuscitation. Today they learn those consequences on real patients. Existing practice tools test one decision at a time (Q-banks) or simulate one short acute encounter (Body Interact, Full Code, Oxford Medical Simulation). None let a trainee treat a patient and then live with the course over days, on local drugs and protocols, at local prices.

## Demand Evidence
None yet. The founder has not spoken to doctors about this. Every demand claim in this doc is a hypothesis until the Assignment is done.

## Status Quo
- Q-banks (UWorld, Amboss, local FCPS/BCS question books): single-decision multiple choice, paid out of pocket.
- Case books and ward rounds: learning on real patients, supervised unevenly, especially on night shifts.
- Sim labs (mannequins): rare, expensive, institution-only.
- Commercial virtual patients: US/EU guidelines and drugs, institution pricing, encounter-length (minutes).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **First user:** BD interns and FCPS/BCS-track residents (medicine, emergency), e.g. an intern at Dhaka Medical College facing a DKA alone on night shift with FCPS Part 1 months away.
- **Budget they already spend:** exam-prep money. The product must be framed as "pass FCPS ward-management / viva" first, "become a better doctor" second.
- **Wedge:** the follow-up loop. Treat at hour 0, then see 6h / day 2 / day 5 consequences of *your* orders, then a debrief against national protocol.

## Constraints
- Content correctness is everything; one wrong outcome kills trust with doctors. Every case is written by one clinician and independently signed off by a second clinician before publishing (two named clinicians per case).
- BD formulary (drugs actually available, local brand/generic names), BD/national guidelines where they exist, otherwise WHO/international.
- Phone-first; patchy mobile data. Must work on mid-range Android over 3G/4G.
- Payment via bKash/Nagad; card-only fails this market.
- Decision data about doctors is sensitive: explicit consent, pseudonymous by default.

## Premises
1. First users are BD interns/residents, not attendings.
2. The wedge is the follow-up loop (treat, then see day 2/5 consequences), not diagnosis chat.
3. **(Revised after second opinion)** Each case is a hand-authored, clinician-signed decision map: orders move the patient to the next state (vitals, labs, nurse note) at fixed time points. AI only voices the patient and phrases feedback, never decides outcomes. A general physiology engine is a later upgrade, built only if authored maps can't cover the combinations users try.
4. Launch with ~10 high-yield ward cases on BD drug lists, not infinite random cases.
5. The "how doctors approach it" dataset is collected with consent from day one but is not the launch pitch.
6. Mobile-first web app (PWA), no app store at launch.

### Approach B: Ward-Round Simulator MVP (CHOSEN)

## Recommended Approach
**Approach B, with demand validation running in parallel.** Build order (below) is chosen so the first playable case exists at the end of M1 (weeks 1–2) and goes to interns while the rest is built; the Validation Gate decides what happens next.

### Core concepts
- **Case:** a patient with a presentation, a hidden truth (diagnosis + severity), and a decision map.
- **Decision map:** time checkpoints (T0 → 6h → day 2 → day 5 → outcome). At each checkpoint the player writes orders; the map picks the next state from rules over those orders (e.g. "insulin started AND no K+ check AND K+ < 3.5 at T0 → day-2 state: arrhythmia").
- **State:** vitals, labs on request, nurse note, patient voice line, and any events.
- **Outcome:** recovered / recovered with complication / deteriorated (ICU) / died, plus a time-to-stabilize number.
- **Debrief:** each order compared against protocol, why it mattered, what peers chose, references.

### User flows (MVP)
1. **Onboarding:** phone OTP sign-in → role (intern / resident / student / other) + specialty + exam target (FCPS P1, P2, BCS, none) → consent screen with separate toggles (see Data & Consent Rules) → first case suggested.
2. **Play a case (core loop):**
   1. Case card: age, sex, chief complaint, setting (medicine ward night shift).
   2. Assess: history snippets, exam findings, order investigations (each costs in-game time and some cost "taka", teaching resource-awareness).
   3. Working diagnosis: pick list (scored against the case's authored correct and acceptable answers) plus optional free text (stored, never scored, shown to authors for review).
   4. Order sheet: fluids, drugs (BD formulary search, dose, route, frequency), monitoring, referral/ICU call.
   5. Advance time → next checkpoint: new vitals/labs/nurse note; patient may improve, worsen, or trigger an event ("Bed 12 is drowsy, GRBS 2.1").
   6. Adjust orders → repeat until day 5 or outcome.
   7. Outcome screen → Debrief.
3. **Debrief:** timeline of your orders vs protocol, 3 key misses/wins, a peer line such as "62% of residents gave KCl before insulin, you didn't" (only shown when the peer-stat threshold is met, else "Not enough data yet"), FCPS-style viva question on the case, share card. All debrief text is pre-authored and clinician-reviewed.
4. **Progress:** cases completed, outcome rate, weak areas by topic (fluids, electrolytes, antibiotics), streak, recommended next case.
5. **Clinician author flow (internal):** create case → define checkpoints and states → set dose bands per drug → write rules + mandatory default state per checkpoint → (optional) LLM drafts patient voice and debrief text → author edits → preview as a player → submit for review → second clinician signs off text and rules → publish. Every published case shows author + reviewer names. Authors see an "uncovered combinations" queue of order sets that fell to a default state.
6. **Paywall:** 2 free cases; monthly/exam-season pass via bKash/Nagad.
7. **Insights (admin, later sold to institutions):** per case, decision distribution, common error paths, outcome by cohort, all on consented pseudonymous data.

### Launch cases (to confirm with clinicians)
DKA, sepsis/septic shock, AKI, acute MI (STEMI, no-cath-lab setting), acute severe asthma, upper GI bleed, hyperkalemia, community-acquired pneumonia, acute stroke, snakebite envenomation (BD-specific).

### Engine Rules
- **Rule matching:** at each checkpoint, rules are evaluated in authored priority order (harm rules first, then treatment-response rules). The first matching rule picks the next state. Every checkpoint must have a clinician-authored **default state** for unmatched order sets; the case cannot be published without one. Every default-state hit is logged to the authors' "uncovered combinations" queue.
- **Orders and doses:** drugs come from the BD formulary. Each case lists the drugs it anticipates, with authored dose bands per drug and route (`none / low / therapeutic / high / dangerous`). Rules read bands, not raw numbers. Two harm lists apply to drugs outside the case's anticipated list: a **global harm list** (e.g. potassium IV push, insulin in hypoglycemia) and a **case harm list** that each author must fill with drugs and drug classes contraindicated for this patient (e.g. NSAIDs and aminoglycosides in the AKI case, non-selective beta-blockers in acute asthma, NSAIDs in upper GI bleed). Matching uses formulary class tags, so any brand in a listed class is caught. A case cannot be published with an empty case harm list, and the reviewer signs it off. A hit on either list moves the patient at the next checkpoint to the harm state authored for that drug or class (e.g. "Hyperkalemic arrest" for IV-push KCl), overriding treatment-response rules. Each global-list entry ships with a default harm state; each case-list entry must have a case-specific harm state. Only drugs on neither list map to "no effect". A dose in the `dangerous` band or a wrong route triggers a one-time in-game nurse/pharmacist challenge ("Sir, 100 units IV? Please confirm."). The player can correct or insist; insisting applies the harm rule.
- **Time model:** within a checkpoint the clock is not continuous. Each investigation is authored as either **bedside** (result now: GRBS, ECG, SpO2, urine dipstick) or **lab** (result appears at the next checkpoint). Ordering more than the case's authored "investigation budget" before first treatment sets a `treatment_delayed` flag that rules can read. Orders take effect from the checkpoint they were written in.
- **Stability and outcome:** each case authors stability criteria (e.g. DKA: pH > 7.3, HCO3 > 15, GRBS < 11). Time-to-stabilize is the first checkpoint whose state meets them, reported as that checkpoint's label (6h / day 2 / day 5 / not stabilized).
- **Where rules run:** on the server. The client receives only the current checkpoint state and the results it has unlocked, never the hidden diagnosis, other states, or the rules. This protects answers and paid cases.
- **Connectivity:** checkpoint payloads are small (target < 20 KB). The PWA caches app assets and the in-progress session; if the network drops, pending order submissions are queued locally and retried, and the player sees "Waiting for connection" instead of losing work. Advancing a checkpoint needs a connection. Fully offline play is post-MVP.
- **No runtime LLM in MVP:** patient voice lines and debrief text are drafted with an LLM at authoring time, edited by the author, and signed off by the reviewer. They ship as fixed text. Nothing the player sees is generated live.
- **Case versions:** every session is pinned to the case version it started on. A safety correction publishes a new version and retires the old one. In-progress sessions on a retired version are ended at their next server call: the player sees what was corrected and why, the session's score is voided (not counted in progress or stats), and they can restart on the new version. Finished sessions keep their scores with a "case since corrected" flag and a link to the correction note. Peer stats are computed per case version.

### Data & Consent Rules
- **Consent screen, separate toggles:** (a) storing your sessions for your own progress — required to use the app; (b) anonymous inclusion in peer comparisons; (c) inclusion in de-identified aggregate reports to medical colleges and institutions, including paid reports; (d) use in research. (b)–(d) are optional and can be changed later.
- **Revoking consent:** turning off (b), (c) or (d) applies to past and future sessions. Peer stats are recomputed nightly from currently consented sessions only, and the user's sessions are excluded from any report or research dataset generated after revocation. Reports and research extracts already delivered cannot be recalled; the consent screen says so. Turning off (a) means deleting the account and all its sessions.
- **No consent to (b)–(d):** sessions are stored only for the user's own progress and scoring, excluded from all peer stats and reports, and deleted when the account is deleted.
- **Thresholds:** a peer stat appears only when ≥ 30 consented sessions exist for that case version. Institutional reports require a cohort of ≥ 10 consented users and never show individuals.
- **Pseudonymization:** phone numbers and payment references live only in an identity table in the auth service. All session and decision data is keyed by a random `player_id`. The analytics/reporting role has no access to the identity table; only the auth service can join them, for account support and deletion.

### Build Order (Approach B, ~6–8 weeks with CC + clinician time)
1. **M1, week 1–2:** case JSON schema, server rule engine, player flow for one case (DKA), debrief, pilot mode. Playable end to end. Used for the Adjust-branch rerun and early clinician review; the Assignment itself is the paper version in week 1.
2. **M2, week 2–3:** clinician authoring tool (rules, dose bands, default states, text, preview, review/sign-off), uncovered-combinations queue.
3. **M3, week 3–4:** phone OTP accounts, identity/decision separation, consent screen, progress page.
4. **M4, week 4–6:** cases 2–10 authored and reviewed (gated on clinicians, not code).
5. **M5, week 5–6:** bKash/Nagad payments and paywall.
6. **M6, week 6–8:** peer stats with thresholds, streaks, share cards. Institution reports are post-MVP.

### Validation Gate (from the paper Assignment, run in week 1 while M1 is being built)
Evaluated on the 5 interns in the paper session. "Finished" = reached the day-5 envelope. "Positive" = said in their own words they would pay for it or use it instead of their Q-bank. Rules are checked in this order; the first that matches applies:
1. **Stop and reframe:** 0 positive → finish M1, then pause and revisit whether the hook is exam prep, ward safety, or institution-led teaching before building more.
2. **Continue full B:** ≥ 3 finished AND ≥ 2 positive → proceed with M2–M6 as planned.
3. **Adjust:** anything else → proceed with M2–M4; defer M5 (payments) and M6 (peer stats, streaks); rerun the pay question with 10 more interns on the playable M1 case.

### Pilot mode (M1 and M2, before accounts exist)
Sessions played before M3 run in pilot mode: no sign-in, a random pilot ID per device, and an on-screen pilot consent ("Your choices on this case are recorded to improve the case. No name or phone number is collected."). Pilot data is used only by the case authors to fix content, never in peer stats or reports, and is deleted when M3 launches.

### Suggested stack
Next.js PWA (asset + session cache) + Postgres (Supabase) with separate identity and decision schemas; case maps stored as versioned JSON validated by a schema; rule engine runs server-side; Claude used only inside the authoring tool to draft text for clinician editing.

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-27, founder input after approval)

- **Open rule engine.** The case format (checkpoints, states, dose bands, harm lists, rules, citations) is a published, versioned spec. Doctors author cases in the authoring tool or import spec-valid JSON through an import API (institutions later). Every contributed case passes the same two-clinician sign-off before players see it.
- **Levels.** Intern, MBBS Final, FCPS Part 1, FCPS Part 2. Each case is tagged with a level. Higher levels add comorbidities, drug interactions, atypical presentations and competing priorities in one patient. Players choose a level; the app recommends one from their exam target.
- **Case sources.** Cases are seeded from openly licensed material (PubMed Central open-access subset, Journal of Medical Case Reports, Cureus, all CC-BY) and adapted by clinicians, with the source credited in the case. No copying from paid Q-banks, FCPS past-paper books, or textbooks. Real patient datasets with restricted licences (e.g. MIMIC) are out of scope.
- **Rewind and replay.** Time advances instantly; nobody waits. At any point after a checkpoint, the player can rewind to an earlier checkpoint, change orders and replay forward. The first complete run is the **scored run**; every rewind creates a **practice branch**. The debrief shows the scored run and each branch side by side ("Branch 2: started KCl before insulin → K+ 4.1 at 6h, no arrhythmia"). Peer stats use scored runs only.
- **Evidence on every lesson.** Each debrief point, harm state and protocol comparison carries a citation: textbook (e.g. Davidson's Principles and Practice of Medicine, edition, chapter, page) and/or guideline (ADA, NICE, WHO, Surviving Sepsis Campaign, BD national/DGHS guidelines) with section and year. Text is paraphrased by the authors; no textbook passages are reproduced. A case cannot be published unless every debrief point has a citation, and the reviewer checks them.
- **Patient names from many ethnicities.** Patient names are generated per session from mixed name pools (Bengali, Bangladeshi Indigenous such as Chakma, Marma and Garo, South Asian, East and Southeast Asian, Middle Eastern, African, European, Latin American and others), matched to the case's age and sex. A name is cosmetic: it never changes clinical facts, rules or outcomes, and it never hints at a diagnosis. A case may pin ancestry only where it is clinically relevant (e.g. G6PD deficiency, sickle cell), and then the author states why.
- **Tone.** Neither boring nor gamified: outcomes still arrive as real ward documents, plus an FCPS-style marks sheet, level progress, and the rewind comparison.

## Success Criteria
- 10 cases published, each signed by two clinicians.
- 100 interns/residents onboarded in the first month after launch, at least 2 teaching hospitals.
- ≥60% of players who start a case reach its outcome screen.
- ≥30% of week-1 users play another case in week 2.
- ≥5% of active users pay within 30 days.
- Clinician reviewers find zero errors they classify as dangerous in a 50-session audit covering outcomes, nurse challenges, patient voice text and debrief text.
